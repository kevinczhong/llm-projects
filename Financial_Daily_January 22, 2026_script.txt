Welcome to the show. Today, we’re tackling a question that’s been puzzling investors and policymakers alike: Why has the dollar not depreciated, even as American political instability seems to be making headlines almost daily?

Let’s start with the basics. Despite the drama in Washington—tariff wars, government shutdowns, and the occasional brush with a debt ceiling crisis—the U.S. dollar remains remarkably resilient. In fact, it’s held up as the world’s preeminent reserve currency, and, if anything, demand for dollars has often increased during periods of global uncertainty.

So, what’s going on? According to Deloitte Insights, the answer lies in a combination of global economic uncertainty and robust U.S. economic fundamentals. When the world gets jittery—be it from trade tensions, geopolitical flare-ups, or even instability in the U.S. itself—investors tend to flock to safe-haven assets. And, for better or worse, the dollar is still seen as the safest port in a storm.

Brookings Institution puts it succinctly: the dollar endures because there simply aren’t credible alternatives. The euro, the yen, and the yuan all have their own structural weaknesses. Meanwhile, U.S. financial markets remain deep, liquid, and—despite the headlines—relatively transparent and well-regulated.

But there’s a paradox here, as Chatham House points out. Sometimes, policies that are meant to weaken the dollar—think aggressive tariffs or loose fiscal policy—end up strengthening it. Why? Because, compared to the rest of the world, the U.S. economy still looks like the cleanest shirt in a dirty laundry basket.

That said, not everyone is sanguine. Fair Observer and the Financial Times both warn that persistent political dysfunction and fiscal brinkmanship are eroding confidence in the dollar’s long-term reliability. Fund managers are increasingly wary of erratic policymaking and threats to the independence of key institutions like the Federal Reserve.

Investopedia and the Associated Press have reported on moments when the dollar has wobbled—episodes where investors briefly lost trust, leading to simultaneous sell-offs in equities, bonds, and the greenback itself. These are reminders that the dollar’s safe-haven status is not immutable.

Yet, as NashFact and FinancialFocusHub remind us, the dollar’s dominance is underpinned by six pillars: economic size, financial market depth, trust in institutions, network effects, military power, and, crucially, the lack of a viable alternative. Even as political risks mount, these foundations have so far proven remarkably resilient.

So, to sum up: American political instability is a risk, and it’s one that global investors are watching closely. But for now, the dollar’s unique combination of economic strength, institutional depth, and global entrenchment means it remains the world’s currency of choice—even in turbulent times.
