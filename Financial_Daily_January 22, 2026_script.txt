Welcome to the programme. Let’s turn our attention to Europe, where equity markets have been on a veritable rollercoaster, driven by the latest twists in US trade policy.

On Monday, January 19th, European bourses were rattled by President Trump’s renewed threats of tariffs targeting eight European countries—a move that sent shockwaves through global markets. The DAX in Frankfurt closed down 1.3%, Paris’s CAC 40 shed 1.9%, and London’s FTSE 100 slipped 0.4%. Investors, spooked by the spectre of a fresh transatlantic trade war, sought refuge in safe-haven assets. Gold and silver both surged to record highs, underscoring the depth of market anxiety.

But the mood shifted dramatically just two days later. On Wednesday, January 22nd, President Trump abruptly reversed course, scrapping the planned tariffs. The relief was palpable. European equities staged a robust rally: the DAX climbed 1.28%, the CAC 40 rebounded nearly 1%, and the pan-European Stoxx 600 advanced 1.1%. The rally was broad-based, with cyclical sectors—those most exposed to global trade—leading the charge.

This sharp swing in sentiment highlights the extraordinary sensitivity of European markets to geopolitical developments and policy uncertainty. Investors are clearly on edge, with trade rhetoric from Washington dictating the tempo of trading in Frankfurt, Paris, and beyond.

For now, the threat of tariffs has receded, but the episode serves as a stark reminder: in today’s interconnected markets, political headlines can move billions in a matter of minutes. We’ll be watching closely to see whether this newfound optimism proves durable—or whether further volatility lies ahead.
